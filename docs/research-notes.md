# Research notes

For each source: what it says, and what it changed in the app. Sources marked *unverified* are cited from memory. Check the details before quoting them.

| Source | What it says | What it changed in the app |
|---|---|---|
| **CMAI Instruction Manual** (Cohen-Mansfield, 1991), provided by the lab | 29 behaviors, each rated 1 to 7 for how often it happened over the last 2 weeks. Codes 8 and 9. Agitated / not agitated thresholds. A summed total is "not useful" (p.5). Inter-rater agreement of .88 to .92, counting ratings within 1 point as agreeing (p.7). | The whole scoring spec. Conversion from counts to ratings, the day-14 review, codes 8 and 9, the agitated badges, total labeled secondary. |
| **CMAI summary table**, provided by the lab | 20 minutes to complete. Often used to measure change after non-drug interventions. Recommends reporting behaviors of interest over domain totals, with a worked example showing a total of 33 to 30 "doesn't tell me much." | The per-behavior chart comes first. |
| Cohen-Mansfield, Marx & Rosenthal (1989). *A description of agitation in a nursing home.* J Gerontol 44(3): M77 to M84. Cited by the manual as the reference for the CMAI. | The original description and validation of the instrument in a nursing home. | It's the citation for the app's About text. It's also why the nursing-home factor set is the default. |
| Cohen-Mansfield, Marx, Werner & Freedman (1992). *Temporal patterns of agitated nursing home residents.* Int Psychogeriatrics 4(2): 197 to 206. Cited in the manual's references. | Agitation follows patterns across the day. | Every log keeps its exact time, and the Scores screen has a time-of-day chart. |
| Cohen-Mansfield (1996). *Conceptualization of agitation: results based on the CMAI and the Agitation Behavior Mapping Instrument.* Int Psychogeriatrics 8(3): 309 to 315. Cited in the manual's references. | Compares the CMAI (caregiver looks back) with direct observation of behavior as it happens. | Event logging is closer to direct observation than to the CMAI. That's why the app needs a stated conversion rule and a caregiver review step. |
| Kales, Gitlin & Lyketsos, the **DICE** approach (Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate). J Am Geriatr Soc 2014; BMJ 2015. *Unverified.* | A structured, non-drug way for caregivers and clinicians to handle behavior symptoms of dementia: describe the behavior, look for causes, try a change, then check whether it worked. | The optional "what happened just before" field (Describe and Investigate). Period-by-period scores support the Evaluate step. This connects the app to the lab's goal of reducing medication. |

## Questions these sources raise for the lab

- Is the app for nursing-home staff or family at home? The manual uses different factor sets for each (both on p.7; the community factors continue on p.8), and there are separate community and relative forms.
- Will caregivers also fill out a paper CMAI at day 14? Comparing it with the app's ratings, using the manual's "within 1 point" standard, would show whether counting gives the same answer as looking back.
