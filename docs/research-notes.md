# Research notes

One row per source: what it says, and what it changed in the app. Manual page numbers are printed page numbers, which equal PDF page numbers in the lab's copy (see `scoring-spec.md`). DOIs were resolved through Crossref on 2026-10-22; none has a retraction or correction notice. Sources marked *unverified* have no checked DOI. Check them before quoting.

| Source | What it says | What it changed in the app |
|---|---|---|
| **CMAI Instruction Manual** (Cohen-Mansfield, 1991), provided by the lab | 29 behaviors, each rated 1 to 7 for how often it happened in the 2 weeks before the rating (p.2). Codes 8 and 9 (p.3). Agitated thresholds (p.5 to 6). A summed total is "not useful" (p.5). Inter-rater agreement .88 to .92, counting a 1-point gap as agreement (p.7). | The whole scoring spec: count-to-rating rule, day-14 review, codes 8 and 9, agitated badges, total labeled secondary. The p.7 standard also sets the change marker (below) and the agreement study's pass mark. |
| **CMAI summary table**, provided by the lab | Often used to measure change after non-drug care. Report behaviors of interest, not totals: a total going from 33 to 30 "doesn't tell me much." | Per-behavior chart comes first. |
| **CMAI terms of use**, Dementia Outcomes Measurement Suite (UNSW). URL: https://dementiaresearch.org.au/doms/behaviour | "The CMAI is copyrighted but can be used free of charge for non-commercial purposes." Other uses need the author's permission. Forms say "All rights reserved" (manual p.23 to 35). | Added `NOTICE` (CMAI content not under MIT). Item descriptions rewritten in our own words with a manual page link. Ratings labeled "CMAI-based (app-derived)". |
| Cohen-Mansfield, Marx & Rosenthal 1989, J Gerontol 44(3):M77-M84. DOI 10.1093/geronj/44.3.M77 | Original description of the CMAI in a nursing home. | Citation for the instrument. Nursing-home factor set (p.7) is the default. |
| Cohen-Mansfield, Marx, Werner & Freedman 1992, Int Psychogeriatr 4(2):197-206. *Unverified* (from the manual's reference list) | Agitation follows patterns across the day. | Every log keeps its exact time; Scores has a time-of-day chart. |
| Cohen-Mansfield 1996, Int Psychogeriatr 8(3):309-315. *Unverified* (from the manual's reference list) | Compares the CMAI (look-back) with direct observation. | Event logging is closer to observation than to the CMAI, so the app needs a stated conversion rule and a review step. |
| **DICE**: Kales, Gitlin & Lyketsos, J Am Geriatr Soc 2014;62(4):762-769 (DOI 10.1111/jgs.12730) and BMJ 2015;350:h369 (DOI 10.1136/bmj.h369) | Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate: a structured non-drug process for behavior symptoms of dementia. | The optional "what happened just before" field (Describe, Investigate). Period-by-period scores support Evaluate. |
| **MCID**: Liu et al. 2025, Alzheimers Dement (N Y) 11(2):e70099. DOI 10.1002/trc2.70099, PMID 40376142. Meunier et al. 2024, Front Neurol 15:1379062. DOI 10.3389/fneur.2024.1379062, PMID 39108660 | Meaningful change in the CMAI total: about 4 points over under a month and 11 points over 1 to 3 months (Liu, independent). 20 points within one patient, range 15 to 25 (Meunier, drug-sponsor funded). Both come from interview ratings, not logs. | Total stays secondary and is labeled app-derived, not comparable to a trial total. A reference band on the total is proposed, not built (see README). |
| **Smith et al. 2025**, Front Dement 4:1607566. DOI 10.3389/frdem.2025.1607566, PMID 41141359 | 30 interviews: 10 of 14 physicians and 10 of 13 professional caregivers said a 1-point change on one behavior could matter. People judge change behavior by behavior. | Per-behavior change marker: 2+ points, or 1 point the same way two periods running. A single 1-point move is inside rater disagreement (p.7), so it is not marked. |
| **AVP-786 Phase 3 protocol** (Otsuka, NCT04464564, v3.0 2023). URL: https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/64/NCT04464564/Prot_000.pdf | "A CMAI caregiver diary will be provided" to support the 2-week CMAI interview. Same rater every visit. | Frames the app as a diary that supports the 14-day rating, not a replacement. Keeps the day-14 review. Rater print view proposed, not built. |
| **Missed logs**: EMA compliance 81.9% in adults (Williams 2021, DOI 10.2196/17023); 86.4% in people 60+, lower past 7 days (Yao 2023, BMJ Open 13(7):e069523, *DOI unverified*). In ReACT 80% of caregivers never used the app (Øksnebjerg 2020, DOI 10.2196/14694). CareHeroes: 19 behavior-checklist submissions in about 19 months (Ruggiano 2024, DOI 10.2196/57308, PMID 39727199). Plus our own simulation (`docs/agreement/robustness.md`) | Missed logs are normal. In the simulation they only push ratings down: at 30% missed, 36 to 42% of items drop a band and a rating of 7 is lost 70 to 82% of the time. | "Nothing to log today" check-in. Unlogged days counted per period and warned about. Rating-7 clusters now cross midnight (`cmai-count-rule-2`). |
| **Placebo response**: FDA Rexulti label, section 14.3 (URL: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/205422s017lbl.pdf); Lee et al. 2023, JAMA Neurol (DOI 10.1001/jamaneurol.2023.3810, PMID 37930669); Grossberg et al. 2020, Am J Geriatr Psychiatry (DOI 10.1016/j.jagp.2019.09.009, PMID 31708380) | Placebo groups fell 17.3 to 17.8 CMAI points in 12 weeks. Drug beat placebo by only 3.8 and 5.3. The CMAI total was the primary endpoint, rated by a clinician from caregiver input. | Total card says totals often fall without any treatment change, and lists the behaviors that drove a change. |

Wider reading (34 sources, including trials, the IPA agitation definition, MDS 3.0 Section E, caregiver burden and HCI studies) is kept outside the repo. It changed nothing beyond the rows above. A recall check found it missed all 11 studies in a Cochrane review of tailored activities (Möhler 2023, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD009812.pub3), because it was scoped to the app's questions, so it does not claim to cover the intervention literature.
